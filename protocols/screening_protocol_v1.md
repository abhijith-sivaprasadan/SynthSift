# Screening protocol v1.0

**Frozen:** 7 October 2026  
**Question:** What drives or limits household/residential adoption, uptake or diffusion of heat pumps?

## Include
A record is eligible when title/abstract-level information indicates that it empirically studies, reviews, or models at least one determinant of **residential heat-pump adoption** or the market/institutional conditions that materially constrain residential deployment. Eligible determinants include economic, behavioural, social, policy, building-stock, information, installer/workforce and infrastructure factors.

Cross-technology heating-choice studies are eligible when heat pumps are a substantive analysed option and results distinguish heat-pump choice or ownership. System-level studies are eligible when the barrier studied plausibly constrains household deployment (for example installer capacity).

## Exclude
Exclude records whose primary contribution is:

1. thermodynamic or component performance without an adoption decision dimension;
2. refrigerant comparison, LCA or environmental performance without adoption analysis;
3. industrial/process/district heat recovery rather than residential adoption;
4. demand response, load shifting or operating optimisation of already-installed equipment;
5. post-adoption comfort/satisfaction alone, unless the study explicitly tests how those experiences affect adoption decisions or diffusion;
6. another heating technology where heat pumps are incidental; or
7. an engineering design/case study with no household adoption content.

## Decision rules
- Screen the source-authored title and abstract/summary where available.
- Do not infer eligibility from full text when the task is title/abstract screening.
- When information is insufficient, retain for human review rather than automate an exclusion.
- Reference decisions are protocol-based pilot labels, **not dual-review ground truth**.
- Any protocol revision requires a new version rather than silently changing v1.0.
